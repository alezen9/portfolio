Faking things is at the heart of game development.

The goal isn't to perfectly simulate reality, but to make something that looks right, feels right,
and fits the world it belongs to. Water is no exception.

There are countless great resources out there on water surfaces: from stylized ponds to hyper-realistic oceans,
each with their own clever techniques.

This isn't meant to compete with them. It's simply my take, my two cents, my story of how I gathered bits of
knowledge from different places and adapted them to my own context.  
In the end, what mattered most was that the water looked and felt good inside my 3D world.

In this article, I'll walk through how my shader evolved step by step: starting with conceptually simple
vertex displacement, moving on to normal textures for more detail, and then pushing things further
with refraction, sun glints, reflections, and a few extra tricks that gave the surface its final character,
all while trying my best to keep the GPU cooler than the surface of the sun. :)

As you can see, the difference is pretty striking, but it didn't happen overnight.  
Each version was a small step forward, built on experiments, mistakes, and whatever bits of
knowledge I managed to piece together.

The water lives inside Revo Realms, my experimental 3D world built with Three.js
([you can explore it here](https://revo-realms.aleksandargjoreski.dev)).  
It's a playground where I test ideas, learn graphics programming, and slowly stitch together an
environment that feels alive.

I really wanted to have water in this world, either a lake or a river, but a lake felt right because it
reminded me of the small one in Masere Park in Pelugo, a small municipality in Northern Italy
where I grew up ([see on Google Maps](https://maps.app.goo.gl/yQj11xUyxqRMamBt6)).
