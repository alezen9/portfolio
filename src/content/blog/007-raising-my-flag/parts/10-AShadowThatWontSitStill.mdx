## A shadow that won't sit still

By the time I came back to the flag, Revo had a brand new shadow system, which I wrote about in
[The Book of Shadows](/blog/paging-my-shadows/). A waving flag with no shadow on a sunny hill looked plain wrong, and in theory
casting one is a single call:

```typescript
graphics.vsmPass.registerCaster(flag, {
  type: "dynamic",
  positionNode: particlePosition.add(vec3(this.origin)),
});
```

`dynamic` means its shadow gets redrawn every frame. The flag's actual mesh is just a flat placeholder plane, because the real shape
only exists in a GPU buffer, so the `positionNode` tells the shadow system where every vertex really is.

The hard part was a different question: which shadow pages does the flag touch this frame? The shadow system answered it with
bounding boxes, which is easy for anything rigid and kept being wrong for cloth. First the box was the placeholder plane at the base
of the pole, so the shadow vanished whenever the cloth swung out of it. Then every small cluster of triangles was bounded by moving
the corners of its box at rest, which ignores whatever the cloth does in between. The new wind bows a row by up to 25 centimetres,
easily enough to cross a page border, so pieces of shadow flickered in and out as the waves travelled across the flag.

{/* illustration F10: a page grid seen from the sun. One row of cloth bowing between its two end points. Left: the box built from the end points, the bulge sticking out of it and the pages it misses shaded red. Right: the box built from every vertex, covering the bulge and all its pages. */}

The fix went into the shadow system: every cluster of every moving object now bounds itself from its real vertices and marks the
pages it covers directly on the GPU, in the same frame it gets drawn. The CPU doesn't need to know where the flag is anymore. The flag
lost its bounds code, about 108 lines disappeared in total, and it now needs around 20 pages instead of 25 to 28. I love when a fix
makes the code smaller :)

It's not free though. A static shadow page is drawn once and cached, while a moving caster's pages are redrawn every frame, 128 x
128 texels each. The flag touches around 20 to 24 of them, about 390,000 texels every frame, against about 49,000 for the ball. That's
about 0.2 to 0.3 ms while it's on screen close up, more than the cloth simulation itself.

A long static shadow is basically free. A big waving one is not.
