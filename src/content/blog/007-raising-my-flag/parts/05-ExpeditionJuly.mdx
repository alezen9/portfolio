## Expedition July

The first version landed in July, a little after midnight.

Now that all the pieces have names, here's what it did. Every frame a compute shader moved the points with Verlet, using gravity, a
constant push along the wind and some sine waves on top to make it flutter, then ran five passes of Jacobi relaxation to keep
neighbouring points at the right distance from each other, with tethers holding it to the staff. The ball could push into it, and there was even some self collision.

It worked. It waved, it reacted to the wind, it looked like a flag.

Eight minutes later I committed again, this time hiding it. Not because it was broken, but because I wanted to share it properly,
after polishing it and writing about it. I just posted a short video on X to tease it a bit.

A month later I tried to improve it, and that went... less well.

The rewrite swapped the solver for a more advanced one, added capsule collisions against the ball's path, aerodynamic drag, three
quality tiers that switched with distance, interpolation between simulation states, a custom lit material and more. It grew to
roughly 1,100 lines and nine storage buffers.

I honestly can't remember exactly why I stopped. Probably a mix of it not behaving the way I expected and it getting too bloated and
complicated to reason about. It stayed on a side branch under a very descriptive commit message: `tmp`.

Looking at it now, most of those ideas are individually sound. The problem was all of them at once. When I came back to the flag this
month, I made one rule for myself: every change has to earn its place with a number, measured on its own, before the next one starts.

In the game, every expedition that doesn't make it leaves its journals behind for those who come after. The July flag and the `tmp`
branch were mine.
