## Where a point goes next

The textbook way to move something is to keep a velocity, add acceleration to it and add it to the position. It's simple, and it
falls apart quickly with anything stiff, like cloth, unless the steps are tiny.

The trick almost everyone uses for cloth in games comes from Thomas Jakobsen's
[Advanced Character Physics](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/academic/class/15462-s13/www/lec_slides/Jakobsen.pdf), a GDC 2001 paper
written for the ragdolls, cloth and plants of _Hitman: Codename 47_. It's called **Verlet** integration, and instead of a velocity it
keeps the previous position:

```typescript
const next = position + (position - previous) + acceleration * dt * dt;
previous = position;
position = next;
```

`position - previous` is how far the point moved in the last step, a velocity in disguise. The beautiful part comes next: if you move
a point to fix something, its velocity changes automatically, with no extra bookkeeping. That makes the next idea very easy.
