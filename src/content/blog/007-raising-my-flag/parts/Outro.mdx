## Closing thoughts

The flag that went up in July and hid eight minutes later is a very different flag now.

It behaves the same at any frame rate, it doesn't stretch like rubber anymore, it's lit, it catches the wind like actual cloth and it
casts a shadow that follows every fold. The simulation costs about 0.065 ms per frame and the shadow 0.2 to 0.3 ms while it's on
screen, and it's all a bit shorter and simpler than the rewrite I parked in August.

The biggest lesson for me wasn't a technique though. It was measuring before believing my eyes. The frame rate problem was invisible on
my laptop, and without that little replica I would still think the July flag was fine.

<br />

There's plenty I didn't do:

- self collision done properly, with a spatial hash instead of checking every point against every other
- a proper bending model based on the angle between triangles, instead of distance constraints two points apart
- more points for finer folds, which is now cheap for the simulation, while the shadow cost doesn't depend on it
- the woven fabric normal map I have sitting on my desktop, for close-ups
- and the newer solvers, like vertex block descent, which pay off with thousands of points, not 425

Each one is a nice rabbit hole for another day.

<br />

And the flag is still hidden. It's behind a feature flag now though, so if you're curious you can already peek at it by adding
`?expedition33=true` to [Revo](https://revo-realms.aleksandargjoreski.dev/?expedition33=true)'s URL. It's on top of a hill,
you can't miss it. Maybe this article is the unhiding.

For those who come after ;)
