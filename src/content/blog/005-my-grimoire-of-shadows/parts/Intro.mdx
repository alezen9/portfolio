It's been almost 2 years since I created the repo and started working on what has become my dearest project,
[Revo](https://revo-realms.aleksandargjoreski.dev/).

Pretty much since it had more in it than just a rolling ball, changing the light's color or position, or moving anything static
in the scene, meant going back to Blender to bake the shadows onto the terrain again.

Then export, pack with the other textures, compress everything to KTX2, load it back into the project and finally sample it in
each object's shader to (incorrectly) darken its color...quite the circus.

And if an object had to shadow itself, like the Goku statue for example, it needed its own bake on its own texture and the
whole dance started again.

Eventually I wrote a few scripts to save some time on the packing and converting part, but even with those, the process was just
long enough that I found myself avoiding changes.

Lighting is such a big part of how a scene feels. I wanted to be able to play with it freely, so a couple of months ago I got
fed up and went down yet another rabbit hole, which led to this:

Before settling on baking, I had tried the built-in shadow map: a directional light, `castShadow`, `receiveShadow` and a shadow
camera following the player around. As the world got bigger and the camera could rotate freely, I kept losing quality or
paying way too much for it, so baked shadows crept in pretty early on.

I also remember giving cascaded shadow maps a try, but I never managed to get them working well, most likely a skill issue on my
end, so back to baking it was.

For a while, the player was the only thing with a real-time shadow and fairly recently I replaced even that with an analytical one
in post. It's a sphere, after all, so the maths was fairly straightforward.

So I kept putting things off. Adding or moving something static meant another bake, and for anything that moved I still hadn't
figured out what to do with its shadow.

<br />

This time I started by looking into how classic shadow maps actually work behind the scenes. I'd been using them since the
beginning, but my idea of what was inside that texture was...pretty far off.

From there I kept digging into the limits of each approach and how other techniques got around them. That took me through
cascades and eventually to **virtual shadow maps**, which I hadn't even known about when I started.

All I wanted was to stop thinking about shadows every time I moved something in the scene. And here I am, yet again, trying to put
the journey into words :)
