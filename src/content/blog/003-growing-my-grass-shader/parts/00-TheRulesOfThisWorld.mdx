## 0. The rules of this world

Before getting into how the system works, it's worth pausing for a moment and describing the world this grass lives in,
because many of the decisions that follow only make sense in that context.

The grass you saw in the video is rendered as a single instanced mesh, driven almost entirely on the GPU. At the time
of writing, it pushes a little over ==8.2 million triangles== just for grass, runs in ==one draw call==, and holds a
==stable 120 FPS== on my M2 MacBook Pro. I did experiment with pushing it further, and while it can go beyond that, I
eventually settled on four segments per blade as a good balance: smooth enough for wind motion, without adding work that doesn't
really show.

Those numbers aren't here as a benchmark or a claim, they're just a snapshot of where the system currently sits, and
they help explain some of the choices ahead.

More importantly, this grass system isn't trying to solve the general case. It takes advantage of a few very specific
rules:

<blockquote>
- The camera has a fixed height and pitch.
- It always stays behind the player.
- The visual direction leans stylized rather than physically accurate.
- Performance is treated as a first-class constraint, not an afterthought.

</blockquote>

These constraints might sound limiting, but in practice they're freeing. Knowing exactly how the camera behaves means
I don't have to design for every possible viewpoint. Knowing that the grass doesn't need to react to dynamic lighting
lets me simplify shading. Knowing that the player will never look straight down or orbit freely removes entire classes
of problems before they even appear.

Once I stopped thinking of these as compromises and started treating them as rules of the world, a lot of decisions
became clearer. The goal wasn't to build the most general grass system possible, it was to build the right one for
this world.

Everything that follows builds on that idea: fewer assumptions, fewer moving parts, and a focus on spending GPU time
only where it actually shows.
