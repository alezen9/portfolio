## Closing thoughts

I can move the sun now and watch every shadow follow it. I can drop a new object anywhere in the scene and it shadows the ground,
the grass and whatever else is around, itself included. For a static object that's one line:

```typescript
graphics.vsmPass.registerCaster(mesh);
```

Moving things register as dynamic, and objects whose shape only exists on the GPU can pass the same deformation they use for
rendering. Custom materials still need to write their direct sunlight, so it's not literally zero setup. It's a whole lot less than
bake, export, pack, compress and wire every time I change my mind though.

{/* media: short clip of the sun moving across the scene, same camera as the intro if possible */}

<br />

It doesn't come for free. The two pools hold 36 MiB of depth, moving shadows are redrawn every frame, a sun change rebuilds the
static pages and the final resolve runs over the whole screen. If the pool ever runs out, a shadow goes missing. The sun can't go too
low. And the grass still doesn't cast. I'm very comfortable with that trade for Revo, and I know exactly where to look when something
breaks, which after two versions of this system is worth a lot to me.

<br />

What I'm happiest about is that I didn't give up this time.

When I was working on the [grass culling](/blog/trimming-my-grass-shader/), I stopped on the prefix sum version. Here, there were
more than a few moments where going back to baking would have been the reasonable thing to do. I kept going, and it works, and it
works wonderfully. It even makes me want to go back and give that prefix sum another try someday.

I also wanted to write all of this down while the detours were still fresh. It took me a while to understand why a visible surface
asks for a page, why an object off screen can fill it, and why that still leaves plenty of ways to get the wrong picture. If you are
staring at a similar shadow problem, I hope going through the ordinary map, the cascades and then the pages gives you a way in. I
certainly needed one.

else in the world, which is exactly why it deserves an article of its own ;)
