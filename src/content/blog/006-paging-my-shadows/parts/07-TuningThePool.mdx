### Tuning the pool

A gentle scene hides a lot, so at some point I also built a stress rig that filled the world with casters of every kind: static ones,
things moved from the CPU and things deformed on the GPU. I did that before most of the improvements in this article, so its numbers
don't say much about Revo today. It did catch a real bug though: a `BatchedMesh` was drawing its whole packed geometry for every
instance, so objects happily cast each other's shapes. Seeing a sphere cast a cube shaped shadow is a special kind of confusing.

My favourite bug from that time didn't need the rig at all. Past the edge of the map there's a large flat terrain plane that
follows the player, so there's always ground under your feet. A flat plane can't cast a shadow on anything, but I had registered it
as a static caster. So every time it moved, the cache dutifully invalidated every page it touched, which was basically all of them,
every frame. In a scripted walk on my M2 Pro it went from 9.84 ms to 7.15 ms of GPU time just by not registering it. The system was
doing exactly what I asked it to do :)

Sizing the pools was simpler. I had given moving casters 400 pages. In normal play the player used somewhere between 5 and
15, so I cut the pool to 64, which took its memory from 25 MiB down to 4 MiB. The static pool stayed at 512 pages.

### The MacBook Air

After merging all of this I tried Revo on a MacBook Air M1. It mostly held 60 fps, but lagged a bit in open grass fields. On my M2
Pro the same field took about 5.4 ms of GPU time standing still and 6.6 ms as soon as I moved, even when I only rotated the camera.
Freezing the shadow system with a debug switch made the difference disappear.

I was wrong twice before I was right:

- **It wasn't the redraws.** The page heat view lit up as soon as I walked, but lowering the shadow resolution cut redraws from
  about 2,000 to about 100 per second and the GPU time didn't move. They were first-time draws of new detail pages, and cheap.
- **The monitoring panel wasn't telling the truth.** It reported 19 ms of render time on a 5.4 ms frame. Apple GPUs overlap
  consecutive render passes, so each pass's timer also counts time spent next to the others. Compute passes don't have that
  problem, so I split the shadow work into named compute passes and timed each one on its own.

That pointed at the page request pass from step 1, **0.8 ms** every frame the view changed. Thousands of pixels were writing the
same few bits at once, and GPUs really don't like many threads writing to one address, so reading the bit first and only writing
when it's still clear took it to **0.5 ms**. What was left was simply reading millions of pixels, and reading one pixel per 2 x 2
block took it to **0.25 ms**.

I refused to believe nobody had solved this better, so I went looking. Unreal and Blender both scan the depth buffer every single
frame. Revo only does it when the view changes, and on a quarter of the pixels. What's left while moving is that scan and the first
draw of pages the camera never needed before, which is simply the price of the view changing. After everything that went into this
system, that felt like a good peak to stop on, even if there's probably a higher one somewhere.

Then I tried the Air again, and it flies. No more stutter in the fields :D

<br />

> A bonus from those days: the "Pages" debug view had not been rendering anything for a while, and I never noticed because a debug
> view that shows nothing looks a lot like a debug view you haven't opened yet. The shader didn't compile. A `select` in TSL gets
> cached the first time it's used, and a later use that needed a different type got the cached variable without the conversion, so
> the shader ended up multiplying an unsigned integer by `0.37`. Replacing that `select` with a plain `mix` fixed it.
